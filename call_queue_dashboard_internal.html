Round-robin ring time impact: The queue uses round-robin routing — calls ring each agent for 20 seconds before moving on. If the 30s SLA timer starts at queue entry, a single no-answer consumes 20 of the 30 seconds, leaving only 10s for the next agent to pick up. With agents lost from early shifts, the remaining agents are further back in the round-robin cycle, increasing the likelihood of the 30s window expiring before connection.

Open questions — pending client response

The following questions need answers from the client before performance can be fully attributed. Until confirmed, the data alone cannot distinguish staffing gaps from routing or configuration issues.

Question 1  ·  SLA definition
When does the 30-second timer start?
If it starts at IVR entry rather than queue entry, IVR time eats into the window before any agent is even rung. This would mean the true agent-available window is shorter than 30s and hitting the SLA may be structurally difficult regardless of staffing.
Question 2  ·  IVR gap
Is there a delay between a call leaving the IVR and entering the agent queue?
Any gap here further compresses the answerable window. Even a few seconds of routing handoff time could account for borderline misses, especially on the 6–8am slots where performance is already marginal.
Question 3  ·  Client staffing
How is the client staffed on their side, particularly 6–9am and weekends?
This is a shared queue. If the client has fewer agents available in early morning windows, the round-robin will spend more ring time on unavailable agents before reaching ours — or vice versa. We cannot assess our team's contribution without knowing their staffing profile.
Question 4  ·  Anomaly dates
What caused the full-day 0% performance on May 16 and May 23?
Both are Saturdays. 0% across all hours with meaningful call volume is not a staffing issue — it is a routing or system failure. Until the client explains these dates, they should not be included in any performance assessment of our team.
Question 5  ·  Routing config
Is the round-robin configuration optimal for a shared queue with a 30s SLA?
At 20s ring time per agent, a single unanswered call uses 67% of the SLA window. If the first agent in the round-robin doesn't answer, the SLA is almost certainly missed. Shorter ring times or priority routing during peak hours may be worth exploring with the client.
