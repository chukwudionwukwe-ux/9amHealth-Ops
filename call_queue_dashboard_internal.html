Binary SLA — the 30s pass/fail problem

Weekday % within 60s is 97.07% — above the 95% target. This means the majority of weekday SLA misses are calls answered between 31–60 seconds, not calls going unanswered. 52 weekday slots that fail at 30s pass at 60s.

The current SLA metric is binary — a call answered at 32 seconds counts as the same miss as a call that rang for 5 minutes. This means the % within 30s figure may overstate the true severity of the performance gap. The 30s vs 60s comparison shows that most misses are late answers, not unanswered calls. This is particularly relevant to the open question about when the 30s timer starts — if IVR time or handoff delay is consuming part of the window, calls may be reaching agents with only 10–15 seconds remaining, making the 30s threshold structurally difficult to meet regardless of staffing.

Question 1  ·  SLA definition
When does the 30-second timer start?
The 30s vs 60s gap (4.92% overall, 4.95% weekdays) suggests many calls are being answered just past 30s. If the timer starts at IVR entry, the effective agent window may be 10–20 seconds — making the threshold structurally difficult to meet.
Question 2  ·  IVR gap
Is there a delay between a call leaving the IVR and entering the agent queue?
Even a few seconds of handoff time compresses the window. Combined with a 20s ring time per agent, the math may make 95% within 30s nearly impossible without routing changes.
